User guide

Two macros,one register.

Decision Register for Confluence keeps the record of a decision on the page where the decision was actually made — and still gives you one table of all of them.

1. What you get

Two macros.

  • Decision — one per decision, on the page where the decision was actually made.
  • Decision Register — one table of every decision you are allowed to see, across pages and spaces.

No admin setup, no configuration screen, no external account.

2. Record a decision

  1. Edit a page and type /Decision, then choose Decision.
  2. The configuration panel opens automatically. Fill in the fields below.
  3. Publish the page. The decision card renders with the status, ID and reversibility as badges.
Fields on the Decision macro
FieldNotes
Decision (one sentence)The call itself, for example Use PostgreSQL for the billing service. Up to 200 characters.
StatusProposed / Decided / Superseded / Rejected.
ID (optional)Leave blank and the app generates a stable ID such as DR-7K3Q2A. Type your own ADR number (for example ADR-0042) to keep an existing scheme.
OwnerThe person accountable for the decision.
Decided onThe date the call was made.
Review byWhen it should be revisited. After this date the register shows the decision as Review overdue.
ReversibilityOptional: Reversible (GO) / Needs safeguard (GUARD) / Irreversible (STOP).
SupersedesThe ID of the decision this one replaces. The older decision then shows Superseded by … in the register.
TagsComma-separated, up to 10.
WhyRationale, alternatives considered, consequences. This is the part your successor needs.
LinkOptional RFC, pull request or ticket.

You can put several Decision macros on one page — for example one page per meeting.

3. Build the register

  1. Create a page — Decisions is a good name.
  2. Type /Decision Register and choose Decision Register.
  3. In the configuration panel choose the Scope:
    • This space — decisions in the space that contains this page (the usual choice).
    • Specific spaces — list the space keys, comma-separated, for example ENG, OPS.
    • Every space I can see — organisation-wide.
    Optionally set a Heading.
  4. Publish.

The table shows ID, decision, status, owner, decided date, review date, reversibility and the source page. Above it: counts per status and the number of overdue reviews. You can filter by status, search by text (ID, title, tag, page), sort by any sortable column, and press CSV to open the export.

4. Export for an audit

Press CSV. A dialog opens with the CSV text for the rows currently visible (filters included). Select all, copy, and paste into a spreadsheet or a .csv file.

In this version the export is a copy dialog rather than a file download.

5. How it stays consistent

The page body is the source of truth. When a page containing decisions is viewed, the app re-reads the page and refreshes a small index stored on the page itself, so:

  • A new decision appears in the register shortly after its page has been opened once.
  • Deleting a Decision macro removes it from the register the next time that page is viewed.
  • Nothing is stored outside your Confluence site.

If a decision does not appear in the register: open its page once, then reload the register (indexing can take up to a minute).

6. Permissions and privacy

The register searches with your own permissions. Decisions on pages you cannot view never appear in your register, and yours never leak into someone else’s. The app sends no data outside your Atlassian cloud site and uses no AI services. See Privacy & Security for details.

7. Licensing

  • The Decision Register view requires an active licence on the site.
  • Decision records render with or without a licence — if the subscription lapses, your decisions remain readable on their pages.
  • Pricing and free tiers are the ones shown on the app’s Atlassian Marketplace listing, and billing is handled by Atlassian.

9. Troubleshooting

Symptoms and fixes
SymptomWhat to do
“Decision (not recorded yet)” on the pageEdit the page, select the macro, open its configuration and fill in at least the decision sentence.
The register is emptyOpen one page that has a Decision macro, then reload the register. Check the scope (This space vs. Specific spaces).
A decision is missing from the registerYou may not have permission to view its page; or the page has not been opened since the decision was added.
“Decision Register is not licensed on this site”Ask a site admin to activate the app licence. Existing decision records are unaffected.
“Could not load decisions”Reload the page. If it persists, contact apps@sata-studio.net with the space key and the time it happened.
