1. Where your data lives

Decision Register is a Forge app that runs entirely inside Atlassian’s cloud (eligible for the “Runs on Atlassian” badge). The vendor operates no servers, no database and no analytics of its own.

Data, storage location, and who can read it
DataWhere it is storedWho can read it
The decision itself (title, status, owner, dates, reversibility, supersedes, tags, rationale, link) In the body of your Confluence page, as the macro’s own configuration Anyone who can view that page, under your existing Confluence permissions
A derived index (satadr content property: decision count, statuses, owners, IDs, earliest review date) As a Confluence content property on the same page, inside your own site The app, and anyone who can read the page
Anything else — Nothing else is stored. No key-value store, no external storage, no caches outside your site

Uninstalling the app does not remove your decisions: they are part of the page body. The derived index becomes inert data on your own pages.

2. What leaves your site

Nothing. The app makes no outbound network requests. It has no external.fetch permission in its manifest, sends no telemetry, uses no third-party analytics, and calls no LLM, AI or machine-learning service of any kind. Content is never written to logs.

3. Permissions requested, and why

Forge scopes in the manifest
ScopeWhy it is needed
read:page:confluenceRead the page body (ADF) to re-derive which decisions are actually on the page
read:confluence-props / write:confluence-propsRead and write the derived satadr index property on the page
read:confluence-content.summaryShow page titles and links in the register table
search:confluenceRun the CQL search that collects decisions across pages and spaces

No scope grants access to spaces, users, Jira, or any product the reader cannot already see.

4. Access control

The Decision Register searches as the current reader (asUser). A decision recorded on a page the reader may not view never appears in that reader’s register. The only operation performed with app permissions (asApp) is writing the derived index back onto the page that is being viewed — this never exposes content, and never grants a reader access to anything new.

5. Availability, licensing and data hostage-taking

Decision records render with or without an active licence. If a subscription lapses or a refund is issued, the register view stops aggregating, but every decision remains readable on its page and inside the page’s storage format. The vendor cannot withhold your records.

6. Security posture

  • No vendor-operated infrastructure, so no vendor breach can expose customer content.
  • No secrets, tokens or credentials are stored by the app.
  • Input from macro configuration is length-limited and normalised before use; the register performs no HTML or script evaluation of user content (UI Kit components only).
  • Dependencies: the Atlassian Forge runtime and @forge/* packages only; no third-party runtime services.

7. Support and incident handling

Response commitments

Security reportsSame address, subject marked SECURITY; acknowledged within 24 hours

Because the app stores its data inside the customer’s own Confluence pages, the worst failure mode is that a macro stops rendering — the underlying decisions remain in the page and can be read without the app.

8. Data residency and subprocessors

The app introduces no subprocessors. All processing happens within Atlassian’s Forge platform and the customer’s own Confluence site, and therefore follows Atlassian’s own data residency for that site.
